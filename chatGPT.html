Area Between Curves & Solids of Revolution

Define two functions, choose an interval, explore the enclosed area, then rotate the region around a line. Drag the 3D graph to orbit and use the scroll wheel or trackpad to zoom.

Examples: x^2, sin(x), sqrt(x+2), e^x, 2*x+1

Area between curves

Shaded region between the two functions on [a, b].

The numerical area is approximated by adding thin trapezoids under the distance between the curves: ∫ |f(x) − g(x)| dx on the chosen interval.
02

Solid of revolution

Rotate the region around the selected line and inspect the resulting 3D surfaces.

Drag to rotate the view. Scroll or pinch to zoom; use the graph controls to reset the camera. The colored surfaces mark the boundary of the rotated region.
Function input: type an expression in terms of x; use ^ for powers and standard functions such as sin, cos, tan, sqrt, abs, ln, and exp. Trigonometric functions use radians.
Rotation note: the horizontal-axis view builds inner/outer radius surfaces, including a disk where the region crosses the axis. For the cleanest vertical-axis solid, choose x = c outside the interval [a, b] or at an endpoint; if the axis cuts through the interval, the rotated surfaces can overlap.
