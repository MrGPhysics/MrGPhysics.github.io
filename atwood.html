Atwood's Machine: A Pulley & Two Masses

Two masses hang from a light string over a frictionless pulley. Why does the heavier side fall? How fast does the system accelerate, and how long does it take to cover a given distance? This chapter derives the motion from scratch and lets you experiment with the masses yourself.

1 Theory & Derivation

The setup, the assumptions, and the step-by-step derivation of the acceleration.

What is an Atwood's machine?

An Atwood's machine is a simple device consisting of two masses, m1 and m2, connected by a light, inextensible string that passes over a pulley. Gravity pulls each mass downward with a force equal to its weight, but the string tension couples the two motions together.

Because the string cannot stretch, the two masses are kinematically linked: when one mass moves up by a distance h, the other moves down by exactly the same distance h, with the same speed and the same magnitude of acceleration.

The key assumptions in this chapter:
  • The pulley is massless and frictionless — it has no moment of inertia.
  • The string is massless and inextensible (the tension is the same on both sides).
  • No air resistance or friction in the bearings.
These make the analysis clean: the only thing that matters is the size of the masses.

Fun fact: the Atwood machine was invented by the Reverend George Atwood in 1784 as a laboratory experiment to verify Newton's second law. By choosing masses that differ only slightly, the acceleration can be made small enough to time with a clockwork pendulum.

Setting up Newton's second law

Assume m1 > m2, so that m1 accelerates downward and m2 accelerates upward. Let the common magnitude of their acceleration be a, and let the tension in the string be T.

Apply ΣF = ma to each mass, taking the direction of motion as positive for each:

m1 g − T = m1 a For the heavier mass m₁, moving down: weight − tension = net downward force
T − m2 g = m2 a For the lighter mass m₂, moving up: tension − weight = net upward force

These are two equations in two unknowns (T and a). Adding them eliminates T.

Solving for the acceleration

Add the two equations above:

( m1g − T ) + ( T − m2g ) = m1a + m2a

The tension cancels, leaving:

( m1 − m2 ) g = ( m1 + m2 ) a

Therefore the acceleration of the system is:

a = g · ( m1 − m2 ) / ( m1 + m2 ) Acceleration of an Atwood machine (ideal, massless pulley)

Notice that g is multiplied by a number between 0 and 1, so a is always less than g. If the masses are equal, the numerator is zero and the system stays in balance (a = 0).

Solving for the tension

Substitute the acceleration back into either equation. Using the second one:

T = m2( g + a ) = 2 m1 m2 g / ( m1 + m2 ) Tension in the string (same on both sides for a massless pulley)

The tension is always between the two weights: m2g < T < m1g. It is greater than the lighter weight (so it can pull it up) and less than the heavier weight (so that one can fall).

Kinematics: distance, time and speed

Once we know the constant acceleration a, the motion is described by the standard uniform-acceleration equations. Starting from rest, the distance moved after time t is:

h = ½ a t²   ⟹   t = √( 2h / a ) Time to move a distance h from rest

The speed after falling a distance h is:

v = √( 2ah ) = a t Speed of the system after moving a distance h

These two results power the interactive lab in Section 3: you choose m1, m2 and h, and it reports a and the time t.

Special cases worth remembering

Equal masses (m₁ = m₂)

  • Numerator is zero → a = 0.
  • The system is in balance and does not move.
  • Tension equals each weight: T = mg.

One side negligible (m₂ → 0)

  • a → g · m₁/m₁ = g.
  • The heavy mass free-falls.
  • Tension → 0 (nothing pulls back).
Direction of the answer: In this chapter the sign of a just tells you which way the system moves. If m1 > m2, m1 goes down and m2 goes up. Swap the masses and everything reverses, but the magnitude of the acceleration is unchanged.

2 Worked Examples

Click a problem to expand the full worked solution. Take g = 9.8 m/s².

EXAMPLE 1

Acceleration and tension of a basic Atwood machine

▶

Masses of 5 kg and 3 kg hang from a light string over a frictionless pulley. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.

Step 1 Identify the masses: m₁ = 5 kg (heavier, falls), m₂ = 3 kg (lighter, rises).
Step 2 Use the acceleration formula:
a = g(m₁ − m₂)/(m₁ + m₂) = 9.8 × (5 − 3)/(5 + 3) = 9.8 × 2/8 = 2.45 m/s².
Step 3 Find the tension with T = m₂(g + a):
T = 3 × (9.8 + 2.45) = 3 × 12.25 = 36.75 N.
Step 4 Check: T should lie between the weights (3×9.8 = 29.4 N and 5×9.8 = 49 N). 36.75 N ✓
Answer: a = 2.45 m/s² (5 kg down, 3 kg up); T = 36.75 N.
EXAMPLE 2

How long to fall a distance h?

▶

For the same machine (m₁ = 5 kg, m₂ = 3 kg), the heavier mass is held and then released from rest. How long does it take to fall 1.0 m? What is its speed at that point?

Step 1 From Example 1, a = 2.45 m/s².
Step 2 Use h = ½at² solved for t: t = √(2h/a) = √(2×1.0/2.45) = √(0.816) = 0.904 s.
Step 3 Speed after 1 m: v = √(2ah) = √(2 × 2.45 × 1.0) = √4.9 = 2.21 m/s.
Answer: t ≈ 0.90 s; speed ≈ 2.21 m/s.
EXAMPLE 3

Designing a small acceleration

▶

To time the motion comfortably in a lab, we want an acceleration of about 0.50 m/s² using total mass m₁ + m₂ = 10 kg. What masses should we use?

Step 1 Write a = g·Δm/Σm with Σm = 10 kg:
0.50 = 9.8 × (m₁ − m₂)/10.
Step 2 Solve for the mass difference:
m₁ − m₂ = 0.50 × 10 / 9.8 = 0.51 kg.
Step 3 With m₁ + m₂ = 10 and m₁ − m₂ = 0.51:
m₁ = (10 + 0.51)/2 = 5.26 kg, m₂ = (10 − 0.51)/2 = 4.74 kg.
Answer: m₁ ≈ 5.26 kg, m₂ ≈ 4.74 kg (a mass difference of only ~0.5 kg).
EXAMPLE 4

Working backwards: finding an unknown mass

▶

One mass is 6 kg. When released, the system accelerates at 1.40 m/s². Assuming the 6 kg mass is the heavier one, find the other mass and the tension.

Step 1 Rearrange a = g(m₁ − m₂)/(m₁ + m₂) with m₁ = 6:
1.40 = 9.8(6 − m₂)/(6 + m₂).
Step 2 Multiply through: 1.40(6 + m₂) = 9.8(6 − m₂).
Step 3 Expand: 8.4 + 1.40m₂ = 58.8 − 9.8m₂ → 11.2m₂ = 50.4 → m₂ = 4.5 kg.
Step 4 Tension: T = m₂(g + a) = 4.5 × (9.8 + 1.40) = 4.5 × 11.2 = 50.4 N.
Answer: m₂ = 4.5 kg, T = 50.4 N.
